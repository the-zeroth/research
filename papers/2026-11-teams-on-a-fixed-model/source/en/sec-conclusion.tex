\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On a fixed frontier model, a well-built harness reaches the vendor's own agent and makes it
cheaper, but it does not yet beat it: we tie Codex on GPT-6.2 Sol at max effort on
Terminal-Bench~4.1 while using about 30\% fewer input tokens.

Three results carry the paper. Context engineering pays in cost, not in accuracy: append-only
prompts cut cost by a third to a half, and later levers took off another 7 to 20\%. Structure pays
only where the work splits: one strong owner beat every team on tasks one context can hold, and
same-model second opinions confirmed the shared misreading instead of catching it. Judgment follows
the model: notes, rules and profiles changed what the model did next but not what it chose, while
facts and measurements put in front of it did move the choice.

Widening the gap therefore needs levers of a different kind from the ones we closed:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Evidence, not advice.} Put measurements of the data and the executable consequences
    of each open reading in front of the lead at the decision, the one channel that moved the
    choice.
  \item \textbf{Diversity that does not share the reading.} Same-model voters agreed on the wrong
    reading. The top row of the maintainers' board is a multi-model agent (71.2\%), and GPT-6.2
    Astra scores 5.5 points above GPT-6.2 Sol in Codex; a second GPT model at the reading or
    choosing step is the obvious next test.
  \item \textbf{More draws with a grounded chooser.} A perfect chooser over two draws would add
    about 1.6 tasks of 30 on GPT-6.2. Choosers, GPT or Claude, were right on visible defects and
    wrong on judgment points (5 of 28), so the chooser must rest on executed checks, not opinion.
  \item \textbf{Comparable reports.} A GPU machine for all 66 tasks and five attempts per task
    would match the maintainers' protocol, the bar for submitting to third-party leaderboards.
\end{enumerate}
