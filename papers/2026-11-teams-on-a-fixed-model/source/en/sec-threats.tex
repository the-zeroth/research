\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

Our numbers resolve differences of about two to three tasks out of 30, so the claim we can make is
that no single lever we shipped or rejected moved the held-out score by more than that.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Noise.} A two-against-two read on the unread 30 has a null standard deviation of
    about 1.4, and every single-variable lever we measured there landed within 2 of its base; only
    the change of configuration in \cref{sec:structure} (profile and effort together) exceeded it.
    Smaller real effects, positive or negative, would not show.
  \item \textbf{Many tests on one held-out set.} The unread 30 were never opened, but dozens of
    levers were read against them, and each read leaks a little information
    \citep{dwork2015reusable}. A lever that passed by luck would be adopted by mistake; we adopted
    few, and confirmed the main one over several draws.
  \item \textbf{Sampling driven by quota.} Draws came from a subscription sign-in with a weekly
    quota. Batches paused when it ran low (26 October, 8 November, 20 November), screens ran on a
    cheaper model first, and some arms stopped early once their bar was out of reach.
  \item \textbf{Sign-in, not API.} Almost every run used a ChatGPT (Codex) sign-in. Costs are
    list-price reconstructions from the server's price table, and the API path was checked only by
    a four-attempt smoke test through OpenRouter. Rate limits, latency and caching may differ on the
    API.
  \item \textbf{One machine.} Thirty attempts shared one desktop PC, so attempts ran slower than on
    per-task cloud machines, and verifiers sometimes timed out under load (handled by quiet
    re-grades). The PC has no GPU, so 3 of the 66 tasks never ran.
  \item \textbf{Self-run results.} The Terminal-Bench~4.1 run was run and scored by us, not by AA or
    the Terminal-Bench maintainers. The adapter is public so that it can be reproduced
    (\cref{app:repro}).
  \item \textbf{We built both sides.} The product, the V1 suite and its graders are ours. Blind
    solves caught two grader defects and later audits retired or repaired unfair tasks, but others
    may remain. Our reward-hacking scan is a heuristic, and the two held-out attempts it flagged are
    unjudged.
  \item \textbf{The product moved.} The product changed during the programs, which is why every
    comparison is within one batch. Results on GPT-6.1 need not hold on GPT-6.2, and results on
    Terminal-Bench~4.0 need not hold on 4.1 (one task differs).
\end{itemize}
